Six ideas the app uses
1 • Duration

Opportunity comes first

Adults commonly need around 7–9 hours, while teenagers generally need more. Individual health and circumstances still matter.

2 • Continuity

Hours can hide disruption

Awakenings, breathing problems, pain, noise or stress may make a long sleep period feel unrefreshing.

3 • Timing

The body has a clock

Light, routine and activity influence circadian timing. Sleep at an unsuitable biological time may feel different.

4 • Regularity

Patterns need several days

One night is noisy data. The app waits before describing regularity or factor differences.

5 • Function

The morning matters

Restoration, energy and concentration add information that a duration total cannot provide alone.

6 • Bio Harmony

Context comes before conclusions

Light, timing, caffeine, movement, mental load, comfort and environment can be logged together, but they remain descriptive signals—not measurements of hormones or health.

Boundary

Tracking is not diagnosis

Self-reports can support awareness and conversations. They cannot identify insomnia, sleep apnea or another condition by themselves.

Why not a single sleep score?

Clarity beats false precision.

SleepSphere shows six dimensions separately and explains the calculation. A strong duration estimate should not erase low restoration; a difficult night should not become a moral failure.

The rule: describe patterns, show sample sizes, separate association from cause, and let the user disagree.
When self-guided support is not enough

Speak to a trusted adult or qualified health professional

Repeated breathing pauses or gasping, loud habitual snoring with choking, dangerous daytime sleepiness, persistent severe exhaustion despite enough sleep opportunity, severe restless sensations, or serious emotional distress deserve appropriate professional attention.

Urgent or dangerous symptoms require urgent local help. SleepSphere is not an emergency service.
